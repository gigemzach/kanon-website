Bookkeeping matters for two reasons: you need to know how the business is actually doing, and someone has to prepare a tax return from it. Books that fail the first test make the second one expensive.

The difference here is that the person keeping your books is the same CPA preparing your return. Nothing gets recorded in a way that creates a problem nine months later, because the person recording it is the one who will have to deal with it.

## Monthly bookkeeping

- Transaction categorization and coding to a chart of accounts that reflects how your business actually works
- Bank, credit card, and loan reconciliations — reconciled properly, not plugged
- Accounts receivable and payable tracking
- Payroll journal entries and reconciliation to your payroll provider
- Fixed asset and depreciation schedules maintained through the year rather than reconstructed in March
- **A monthly financial package** — profit and loss, balance sheet, and cash flow, with a short written note on what changed and why. Not a data dump.
- Quarterly check-in on estimated taxes so the April number is never a surprise

## Cleanup and catch-up

This is often the reason people call. Common situations:

- Books that were never reconciled, or reconciled to a balancing plug
- A chart of accounts that grew organically and no longer maps to anything useful
- An accounting system migration that landed balances in the wrong places
- Cash-basis records that need to be put on accrual for a lender, an investor, or a buyer
- Several years behind, with returns that cannot be filed until the books are current

Cleanup is scoped and quoted after a diagnostic review. The honest answer to "how long will this take" is unknowable until someone has looked, and we would rather tell you that than guess.
