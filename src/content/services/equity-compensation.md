Equity compensation is where otherwise straightforward returns go wrong. The award agreement is written by lawyers, the brokerage statement reports a cost basis that is frequently incomplete, and the withholding your employer applied was a flat supplemental rate that may bear little relationship to your actual marginal rate.

The result is predictable: a large balance due in April on income you already saw withheld, or a materially overstated capital gain because nobody adjusted basis for the amount already taxed as wages.

## What we handle

- **RSUs and PSUs** — income recognition at vest, the supplemental withholding shortfall, and basis correction on the subsequent sale so the same dollars are not taxed twice
- **Incentive stock options** — the spread at exercise, alternative minimum tax exposure, the disqualifying disposition rules, and AMT credit recovery in later years
- **Non-qualified stock options** — ordinary income at exercise, employer reporting, and the Form 1099-B basis mismatch that follows almost every cashless exercise
- **ESPP** — qualifying versus disqualifying dispositions and the ordinary income component most brokerages do not report
- **Section 83(b) elections** — the 30-day window, when the election is worth making, and the filing mechanics
- **Multi-year and multi-state awards** — grants that vest after you moved, and the sourcing rules that decide which state gets to tax them

## When to call

Before the transaction, if there is any way to manage it. Most of the value in equity comp work is in sequencing and timing, and almost none of it is available after December 31. An exercise decided in November and a return filed in April are two very different engagements.

If you are already past the event, the work is damage assessment and accurate reporting — still worth doing well, since basis errors on equity sales are among the most common and most expensive mistakes on high-income returns.

## What this is not

We advise on the tax consequences of equity decisions. We do not advise on whether to hold or sell a position, we do not manage assets, and we are not a registered investment adviser. If you work with a financial adviser, we are happy to coordinate — the tax analysis usually improves their recommendation and vice versa.
