This is the foundation of the practice. Most clients come here for a return that is prepared correctly, filed on time, and explained in language that makes sense.

## Who we prepare returns for

- **Individuals and families** — Form 1040, including multi-state filers, self-employment income, rental property, and investment activity
- **Small businesses** — partnerships (1065), S corporations (1120-S), and C corporations (1120)
- **Estates and trusts** — fiduciary returns (1041)
- **Texas businesses** — franchise tax reports and public information reports, including the no-tax-due filings that still have to be submitted

## What's included

Every return engagement covers:

- **Preparation and e-filing** of federal and all applicable state returns
- **A prior-year review.** We read last year's return before preparing this one. Errors and missed items are more common than people expect, and an amended return is often worth filing.
- **Identification of the deductions and credits you're entitled to**, based on what actually happened during the year rather than what fits neatly in a software prompt
- **Estimated payment calculations** and a payment schedule for the year ahead
- **A written summary** explaining what drove your result — what changed from last year, and what to expect next year
- **Year-round access.** Questions in July get answered in July. Tax planning that only happens in March is mostly just reporting.

## Why the review matters

The most expensive errors on a return usually are not arithmetic. They are situations where a third-party form reported something one way and the underlying transaction was actually something else — a brokerage that reported a sale with incomplete cost basis, a K-1 allocation that does not match the operating agreement, a 1099 issued to the wrong entity.

Software will accept all of it without comment. Catching it requires someone reading the documents against what you tell them happened. That is most of what you are paying a CPA for.
