An IRS notice arrives with a deadline, a proposed adjustment, and no explanation of how the number was calculated. Most people either pay it or ignore it. Both are frequently the wrong move.

A significant share of automated notices — CP2000 underreporter notices in particular — are generated because a third party reported something the IRS could not match to your return, not because you owe more tax. Broker basis reporting and equity compensation produce these constantly.

## What we handle

- **CP2000 and underreporter notices** — reconstructing what was actually reported and responding with substantiation
- **Balance due and collection notices** — establishing the correct liability first, then addressing payment
- **Examination correspondence** — responding to document requests and correspondence audits
- **Unfiled returns** — bringing multiple years current, in the right sequence
- **Penalty abatement** — first-time abatement and reasonable cause, where the facts support it
- **Installment agreements** — when the liability is correct and the issue is cash flow

As a licensed CPA, we are authorized to represent taxpayers before the IRS.

## A note on expectations

We do not advertise settlements for a fraction of what you owe, and you should be skeptical of anyone who does. What we can tell you, usually within one conversation, is whether the notice is correct — and if it is not, what the correct number is and how to demonstrate it.

If you have a notice with a response deadline, that deadline matters. Send it over before it passes.
